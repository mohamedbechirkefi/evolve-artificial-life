## Part B. Confirmatory protocol proposal

### B.1 Research question

Within a fixed budget of policy evaluations, does evolutionary selection with inherited, mutated policies find foraging policies with higher fitness on held-out grid environments than independent random search, and does any advantage survive changes in budget, mutation strength and training-set size?

### B.2 Hypotheses (primary, EXP-001)

Let `d_s = F_test(EVO, s) - F_test(RS, s)` be the paired difference in held-out test fitness for run seed `s`, and `delta = E[d_s]` over run seeds.

- **H0:** `delta <= 0`. Evolutionary selection does not produce higher held-out fitness than random search at equal evaluations.
- **H1 (directional):** `delta > 0`.
- **Decision rule:** H1 is supported if the lower bound of the two-sided 95 percent paired bootstrap confidence interval for `delta` is above 0. H1 is contradicted if the upper bound is at or below 0. If the interval contains 0, the experiment cannot resolve the question; this is not evidence of equivalence.

### B.3 Formal model and operational definitions

- **Environment.** A 16 x 16 toroidal grid. Each environment instance places `n_food = 24` food items on distinct cells, sampled from an environment seed; food clusters are generated around 3 centres with a fixed spread (generator parameters in `config.json`). The agent starts at the centre with a random heading drawn from the environment seed. An episode has `T = 100` steps.
- **Observation.** A 5 x 5 egocentric window, rotated to the heading, containing a food indicator per cell (24 values, own cell excluded), plus a constant bias input.
- **Actions.** `{forward, turn_left, turn_right, stay}`. Moving onto a food cell consumes it; a consumed item never reappears within the episode.
- **Policy.** A deterministic feed-forward network: 25 inputs, 8 tanh hidden units, 4 outputs, argmax action. Parameter vector `theta` of dimension 25*8 + 8*4 + 4 = 236 (hidden biases folded into the bias input; exact count fixed in code and checked by test). Initial parameters `theta ~ N(0, 1)` per component.
- **Episode fitness.** `f(theta, e) = number of food items consumed in environment e`, in items, bounded in `[0, 24]`.
- **Training fitness.** `F_train(theta) = (1/K) sum_k f(theta, e_k)` with `K = 5` environments drawn, for each evaluation, from the training seed pool.
- **Held-out test fitness (primary metric).** `F_test(theta) = (1/M) sum_m f(theta, e_m)` over a fixed set of `M = 200` test environments from a seed pool disjoint from the training pool. Same test set for every arm and run (common random numbers). Unit: food items per episode.
- **Evaluation.** One evaluation is one policy scored on K training episodes. Every arm receives exactly `E = 2000` evaluations.
- **Returned policy.** Every arm returns the policy with the highest training fitness among all policies it evaluated (same output-selection rule for all arms). Only that policy is scored on the test set.

### B.4 Arms

| Arm | Definition |
|---|---|
| RS (primary baseline) | Sample E = 2000 independent `theta ~ N(0, 1)`. |
| NOSEL (no-selection control) | Population P = 50, G = 40 generations. Parents chosen uniformly at random (neutral drift), offspring `theta' = theta + sigma * N(0, I)`, `sigma = 0.1`. |
| EVO (evolutionary selection) | Population P = 50, G = 40. Truncation selection: top 10 by training fitness are parents; one elite copied unchanged (re-evaluated, counted in the budget); offspring `theta' = theta + sigma * N(0, I)`, `sigma = 0.1`. |
| SELONLY (mutation ablation, EXP-002) | As EVO with `sigma = 0`: selection acts only on the initial variation. |
| EVO-sigma (EXP-002) | EVO with `sigma` in {0.02, 0.5}. |

RS is the simplest baseline and is the primary comparator. Generation 0 of NOSEL and EVO uses the same 50 draws as the first 50 RS samples for the same run seed (matched starts).

### B.5 Secondary metrics

1. **Adaptation:** best-so-far training fitness as a function of evaluations; area under that curve normalised by E.
2. **Generalisation gap:** `F_train(returned) - F_test(returned)`, where `F_train` is re-measured on 200 fresh training-pool environments to remove the winner's-curse bias.
3. **Shifted-distribution fitness:** `F_shift` on 200 environments from a different generator (uniform food placement, `n_food = 16`), never used for any choice.
4. **Diversity:** mean pairwise Euclidean distance of parameter vectors in the final population (genotypic), and mean pairwise Hamming distance of action sequences on a fixed probe set of 20 training environments (behavioural). RS diversity uses its last 50 samples.
5. **Exploit audit:** fraction of steps spent on `stay`, number of turns in place, and a replay check that consumed count equals the decrease in food on the grid.

### B.6 Experimental unit, seeds and sample size

- **Experimental unit:** one independent run, identified by a run seed. Episodes within a run are not independent units and are averaged before analysis.
- **Seeds:** 20 run seeds per arm (`s = 0..19`), at least the 5 required. Run seeds are paired across arms. Seed derivation uses `numpy.random.SeedSequence` with separate spawn keys for search, training environments, test environments and shifted environments; a test asserts that the training, test and shifted environment seed sets are disjoint and that no test-environment layout hash appears among training layouts.
- **Sample-size rationale (planning approximation):** for a paired two-sided test at alpha 0.05 with 80 percent power, `n = 20` pairs detects a standardised paired effect of about `d_z = (1.96 + 0.84) / sqrt(20) = 0.63` (normal approximation; the t correction raises it to about 0.66). This is a cost-driven choice: runs are cheap, and n will be raised to 30 at freeze if the smoke measurement shows the full matrix fits in under 4 hours.

### B.7 Uncertainty and analysis

- Mean paired difference, 95 percent percentile bootstrap interval over run seeds (10,000 resamples, fixed analysis seed), and `d_z` with its bootstrap interval.
- Per-seed values and raw distributions (strip plots) for every arm.
- Secondary comparisons (EVO vs NOSEL, EVO vs SELONLY, sigma levels) use the same paired bootstrap, reported as estimates with intervals, without a significance claim; they are labelled secondary.
- A Wilcoxon signed-rank test is reported as a robustness check only.

### B.8 Exclusion and stopping rules

- A run is excluded only for a crash, a non-finite value or a failed invariant check. It is rerun once with the same seed; if it fails again it is recorded as a failure, the pair is dropped from the primary analysis, and a sensitivity analysis assigns the failed run the worst observed value of its arm.
- No optional stopping: the seed count is fixed at freeze. No arm, metric or seed is dropped after results are seen.
- Hyperparameters (P, G, sigma, truncation size) are fixed at freeze and are never tuned on test or shifted environments. Any tuning uses training-pool environments only and is reported as exploratory.

### B.9 Matrices

- **Smoke matrix (exploratory, M3 first step):** arms RS, NOSEL, EVO; 2 run seeds; E = 200 (P = 10, G = 20); M = 20 test environments. Purpose: runtime measurement, metric validation, schema validation. Results are never pooled with confirmatory results.
- **Full matrix (after freeze):**
  - EXP-001: RS, NOSEL, EVO x 20 seeds x E = 2000. 60 runs.
  - EXP-002: SELONLY, EVO-sigma0.02, EVO-sigma0.5 x 20 seeds x E = 2000 (EVO sigma 0.1 and NOSEL reused from EXP-001 at the same code revision). 60 runs.
  - EXP-003: RS and EVO at E = 500 and E = 8000 (4 x 20 = 80 runs), and RS and EVO at E = 2000 with K = 1 fixed training environment (2 x 20 = 40 runs). 120 runs.

### B.10 Sensitivity analyses that could invalidate the main interpretation

1. **Budget (EXP-003).** If the EVO advantage disappears or reverses at E = 500 or E = 8000, the primary result is budget-specific and the claim is restricted to E = 2000.
2. **Training-set size (EXP-003).** With K = 1 fixed training environment, selection may overfit. If EVO beats RS on training fitness but not on test fitness in that condition, the main interpretation ("selection improves held-out performance") depends on the training design rather than on selection as such.
3. **Selection versus drift (EXP-002 and EXP-001 secondary).** If NOSEL matches EVO, the improvement is attributable to local mutation search from good starts, not to selection.
4. **Exploit audit.** If high-fitness policies rely on a behaviour that the audit flags as a scoring artefact (Lehman et al. 2018; Krakovna et al. 2020), the result is reported as an exploit, not an improvement.

### B.11 Confounders and controls

Equal evaluation counts, matched run seeds, matched generation-0 populations, a shared test set, an identical output-selection rule, and an identical policy architecture across arms. Evaluations are counted, not wall-clock time; wall-clock is recorded for information.

### B.12 Fallback

If compute exceeds the budget, cut EXP-003's E = 8000 condition first, then reduce the test set to M = 100. Seeds per arm do not fall below 10, and the primary EXP-001 matrix is never cut.
